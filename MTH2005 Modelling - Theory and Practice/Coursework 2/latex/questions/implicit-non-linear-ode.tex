    We are given the non-linear ODE
    \\
    \begin{center*}
        \begin{align*}
            & \frac{dx}{dt} = \sin(x^{3}) && \\
        \end{align*}
    \end{center*}\\
    Let $f(x) = \sin(x^{3})$ and with $\Delta t = 0.1$ gives.
    Then, with Backward-Euler $x_{n+1}$ becomes
    \\
    \begin{center*}
        \begin{align*}
            & x_{n+1} = x_{n} + \Delta t f(x_{n+1}) && \\
            & x_{n+1} = x_{n} + \Delta t \sin(x_{n+1}^{3}) && \\
        \end{align*}
    \end{center*}\\
    Since $x_{n+1}$ is within the $\sin$ component of the equation, we cannot rearrange this
    so that $x_{n+1}$ is the subject, hence the method is implicit and instead must be solved
    using
    \\
    \begin{center*}
        \begin{align*}
            & g(x_{n+1}) = x_{n+1} - x_{n} - \Delta t \sin(x_{n+1}^{3}) = 0 && \\
        \end{align*}
    \end{center*}\\
    This is a non-linear equation and hence we need to iterate using
    the Newton-Raphson method to solve $g(x_{n+1}) = 0$:
    \\
    \begin{center*}
        \begin{align*}
            & z^{(k+1)} = z^{(k)} - \frac{g(z^{(k)})}{g'(z^{(k)})} \quad \text{with our initial guess } z^{(0)} = x_{n}.&& \\
            & g(z) = z - x_{n} - \Delta t \sin(z^{3}) && \\
            & \implies g'(z) = 1 - 3z^{2} \Delta t \cos(z^{3}) && \\
        \end{align*}
    \end{center*}\\

    $t \in [0, 5]$ with $\Delta t = 0.1$ gives:
